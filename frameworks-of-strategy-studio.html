What the chart covers

The chart maps named, reusable strategy frameworks from 1956 to 2025: models, matrices, typologies and decision procedures that structure a strategic choice and that practitioners and teachers use by name. It is a curated reference, not a census of the strategy literature. General theories and research programmes appear only where they gave practice a usable framework (for example, the resource-based view).

How entries were selected

An entry had to meet all three tests:

  1. Named and reusable. The idea is known by a name and can be applied to a new case, rather than being a single finding or a general exhortation.
  2. Traceable origin. It can be traced to an originating publication: a book, a practitioner journal article (HBR, Sloan Management Review, California Management Review), a peer-reviewed article, or a consultancy publication.
  3. Uptake. It is widely taught in MBA or executive programmes or widely used by practitioners. For frameworks published from 2015 onwards, evidence of uptake within the decade was required.

Selection is editorial judgement informed by these tests. It is not a systematic review and no citation-count threshold was applied.

Three kinds of entry

  • BCG 2015 timeline (). Every framework in BCG's interactive The History of Strategy (2015), which is the starting point.
  • Restored omissions (). Frameworks published before 2015 that meet the tests but are absent from BCG's chart, notably non-market, international and behavioural strategy.
  • Added 2015–2025 (). Frameworks published after BCG's chart, chiefly on platforms, ecosystems, AI, resilience and purpose.

Dating

Each framework is placed at the year the idea was first published, not the year it became popular. Where an article preceded the best-known book, the article's year is used and both works are listed.

Approach tags

Each entry carries one tag from BCG's Strategy Palette (Reeves, Haanæs and Sinha, Your Strategy Needs a Strategy, 2015): Classical, Adaptive, Visionary, Shaping or Renewal, plus Meta for frameworks that help choose among approaches. BCG's tags are kept for its entries; the editor assigned tags to the rest using the Palette's definitions. A single tag simplifies frameworks that serve more than one approach.

Status labels

Core set (): frameworks taught in a full executive programme. Library (): used when a case or client calls for them. Historical (): superseded, absorbed into a successor or rarely used. Operations (): operational-excellence tools kept for completeness. These are teaching judgements, not measures of scholarly importance.

Lineage and pairings

A lineage link from A to B means that work B explicitly builds on, extends or responds to work A (for example, by naming and developing it), as judged by the editor from the works themselves. Thematic similarity alone does not create a link, so many plausible influences are deliberately left out. "Pairs with" is a teaching suggestion, not a claim of intellectual descent.

Verification

Years, originators and descriptions were checked against original sources in independent review passes in October 2026, and the references below were checked against publisher and journal records in November 2026. A DOI is shown only where it was confirmed; most books and HBR articles have none. Descriptions are the editor's own summaries.

Evidence ratings

Each framework is rated strong (systematic empirical tests, broadly supportive), mixed (tests exist but results are contested or context-dependent), limited (mainly cases, practitioner experience or the authors' own data) or conceptual (a heuristic or design tool not framed for testing). The main published critique is given where one was identified and its reference confirmed. Ratings are the editor's judgement of the literature, not a systematic review, and they rate the empirical support for a framework's claims, not its usefulness in practice.

Limitations

The sources lean towards Anglo-American scholarship and towards frameworks popularised by consultancies (BCG, McKinsey, Bain) and Harvard Business Review. Frameworks from other traditions, and work published in languages other than English, are under-represented. The core set reflects one executive curriculum. Corrections and suggestions are welcome.
